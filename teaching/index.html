MS&E 176: Social Entrepreneurship

Instructor and Course Designer · Spring 2025, Spring 2026

Designed and developed this course for Stanford’s Department of Management Science & Engineering.

Course Description

This course introduces the fundamentals of social entrepreneurship as a process for developing ventures that address complex social challenges. Students learn how social entrepreneurs identify problems, design solutions, mobilize resources, and build organizations that balance social impact with financial sustainability.

The course emphasizes practical decision-making under constraints and the role of capital, partnerships, and ethics in scaling impact-driven ventures. Students explore how an entrepreneurial mindset can be applied to social problems by identifying opportunities for innovative organizational and market-based solutions. This perspective is valuable for launching social ventures and driving change within corporations, nonprofits, government, and the investment community.

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  1. Articulate a process for identifying a social problem and translating it into a viable social venture opportunity.
  2. Design and justify a social impact business model, including the choice of organizational form and theory of change.
  3. Evaluate and select financing strategies for social enterprises, considering nonprofit, hybrid, and for-profit capital sources.
  4. Analyze tradeoffs between mission, money, risk, and scale in social venture decision-making.
  5. Develop and deliver an investment pitch that clearly communicates a venture’s problem, solution, impact, and funding strategy.
