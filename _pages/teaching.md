## Stanford University

### MS&E 176: Social Entrepreneurship

**Instructor** · Spring 2025 and Spring 2026

<div class="teaching-highlight">
  I developed Social Entrepreneurship from the ground up for the Department
  of Management Science &amp; Engineering and serve as its instructor.
</div>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Course Description</summary>

This course introduces the fundamentals of social entrepreneurship as a process for developing ventures that address complex social challenges. Students will learn how social entrepreneurs identify problems, design solutions, mobilize resources, and build organizations that balance social impact with financial sustainability. The course emphasizes practical decision-making under constraints and the role of capital, partnerships, and ethics in scaling impact-driven ventures.

The class demonstrates an entrepreneurial mindset applied to social problems. Social entrepreneurs identify opportunities for innovative organizational and market-based solutions. This perspective is valuable not only for launching social ventures, but also for driving change within corporations, nonprofits, government, and the investment community.

By the conclusion of the course, students will be able to:

1. Articulate a process for identifying a social problem and translating it into a viable social venture opportunity.
2. Design and justify a social impact business model, including the choice of organizational form and theory of change.
3. Evaluate and select financing strategies for social enterprises, considering nonprofit, hybrid, and for-profit capital sources.
4. Analyze tradeoffs between mission, money, risk, and scale in social venture decision-making.
5. Develop and deliver an investment pitch that clearly communicates a venture’s problem, solution, impact, and funding strategy.

</details>
