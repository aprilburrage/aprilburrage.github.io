I’m April Burrage, a Provostial Fellow and Lecturer in the Department of Management Science & Engineering at Stanford University.

**I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.**

My research examines how workplace rules, public policies, and professional networks shape entrepreneurship and innovation. I am particularly interested in how individuals with STEM backgrounds develop ideas, pursue entrepreneurial opportunities, and acquire resources to build ventures in high-growth fields.

Across my research, I ask:

1. How do ownership rights and workplace relationships shape whether individuals develop ideas within existing firms or through new ventures?
2. How do public policies and program design shape access to entrepreneurial opportunities and participation in innovation?
3. How do career experiences and professional networks influence entrepreneurs’ access to resources and opportunities?

In my [job market paper](/job-market-paper/burrage-grennan_job-market-paper.pdf), with [Jillian Grennan](https://www.jilliangrennan.com/), we examine how employer rights over employee inventions affect entrepreneurial entry and the direction of innovation. This research was supported by the Washington Center for Equitable Growth.

My research has been published in *Research Policy*, the *Journal of Economics & Management Strategy*, and *Environmental Research Letters*, and has been cited by the Brookings Institution. My work has received support from the National Science Foundation and the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation.

At Stanford, I designed and teach Social Entrepreneurship, a course on developing ventures that address social challenges.

I received my Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Massachusetts Amherst. Before academia, I worked in financial services and digital media, advising corporate executives, entrepreneurs, and small-business owners.
