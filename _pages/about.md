I’m April Burrage, a Provostial Fellow and Lecturer in the Department of Management Science & Engineering at Stanford University.

**I am on the 2026–2027 job market.**

My research examines how legal rules, organizational boundaries, and professional networks shape innovation and entrepreneurship. Using causal inference, field experiments, and text analysis, I study three interconnected areas:

- **Innovation and intellectual property:** How legal rules and firm boundaries govern the development and commercialization of new ideas.
- **Human capital and high-tech entrepreneurship:** How STEM training and career pathways affect who enters invention and venture creation.
- **Entrepreneurial finance and resource allocation:** How professional networks, venture capital, and public R&D funding determine which technologies and founders access capital.

In my job market paper with [Jillian Grennan](https://www.jilliangrennan.com/), Employee Intellectual Property Rights and Innovation-Driven Entrepreneurship, we combine a quasi-natural experiment and a novel text-based measure of technological distance to examine how stronger employer claims over employee inventions affect entrepreneurship. We find that stronger employer claims reduce inventor founding and lead founders to pursue technologies further from their former employers’ portfolios.

My research has been published in *Research Policy*, the *Journal of Economics & Management Strategy*, and *Environmental Research Letters*. My work has also been supported by the National Science Foundation, the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, and the Washington Center for Equitable Growth.

At Stanford, I designed and teach *Social Entrepreneurship*. I received my Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Massachusetts Amherst, and prior to academia, I worked in financial services and digital media advising corporate executives, entrepreneurs, and small-business owners.
