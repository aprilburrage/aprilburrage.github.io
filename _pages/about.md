I’m April Burrage, a Provostial Fellow and Lecturer in the Department of Management Science & Engineering at Stanford University. I am on the 2026–2027 job market.

My research examines how workplace rules, public policies, and professional networks shape entrepreneurship and innovation. I am particularly interested in how individuals with technical expertise turn their ideas into ventures and acquire resources to develop them.

In my [job market paper](/job-market-paper/burrage-grennan_job-market-paper.pdf), with [Jillian Grennan](https://www.jilliangrennan.com/), we examine how employer rights over employee inventions affect entrepreneurship. We find that stronger employer claims reduce entrepreneurial entry among inventors. We argue that these claims weaken the value inventors can retain from their ideas and may make it harder to attract venture capital, making entrepreneurship less attractive. We also examine how founders’ inventions relate to their prior work and their former employers’ technologies. This research was supported by the Washington Center for Equitable Growth.

My research has been published in *Research Policy*, the *Journal of Economics & Management Strategy*, and *Environmental Research Letters*, and has been cited by the Brookings Institution. My work has received support from the National Science Foundation and the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation.

At Stanford, I designed and teach Social Entrepreneurship, an undergraduate course on developing ventures that address social challenges.

I received my Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Massachusetts Amherst. Before academia, I worked in financial services and digital media, advising corporate executives, entrepreneurs, and small-business owners.
